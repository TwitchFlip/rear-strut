    Procedure

    Unstick the lower separator

    Not a Kress procedure. Get Kress or your supervisor to approve it first, and do it under your site's isolation rules. It runs the engine with a nitrogen valve open, which the manual never does.

    Why it happens (working theory)

    The lower separator (2) sits in the housing under the rod end. When the rear extends hard, for example when the load leaves at the dump, oil can't refill the housing fast enough through the orifices (7). The bottom gas then pushes the separator further up than it normally goes, and it binds there. At ride height the rod end never comes down far enough to touch it, so nothing knocks it free.

    Signs

    • On blowdown the rear stops above 0% while the strut oil reads 0 psi.
    • The bottom nitrogen reads low with the struts blown down.
    • The rear jolts down when raise pressure first reaches the strut.

    Check first

    1. Engine off. Open the manual override on the blowdown solenoid so the struts are open to tank.
    2. Let it settle and read IQAN. If the rear is at 0% with the oil at 0 psi, the separator is free: go straight to Set or reset precharge.
    3. If the rear sits above 0%, the separator is either stuck or being held up by too much gas. Carry on below.

    Steps

    1. Drain the bottom chamber at the scissor-link charge valve with the low-pressure charging assembly (224506). Use the Kress decrease steps: T-handle in until the gauge reads, open the poppet three turns, then open the bleed valve slowly. Run the bleed into an approved container. Both rear struts drain together because their bottom chambers are linked.
    2. Watch the rear. If it comes all the way down as the gas leaves, the separator wasn't stuck. It was being held up by too much gas. Skip to step 7.
    3. Watch the bleed. A little oil is normal (you saw under 100 mL). If oil keeps flowing, the separator seal is leaking and the cylinder needs repair. Stop here.
    4. Keep the drain open into the container and clear everyone from the scissor link and rear end. Start the engine and use manual raise in short bursts. With no gas under it, the oil only has to beat friction to push the separator down.
    5. Read what happens.
      • The pump runs but the rear doesn't lift at first, then starts to rise: the separator has moved down to the bottom and is free.
      • The rear lifts straight away: the separator is still stuck. Stop. The strut needs to come out.
      • Oil comes out of the drain once the separator bottoms: the seal is leaking. Stop. Repair.
    6. Lower the rear fully and shut down.
    7. Open the blowdown override and confirm 0% and 0 psi on IQAN, then recharge with Set or reset precharge. Because the strut is open to tank, the gas lifts the separator back up to the rod end as the oil leaves. That's the position the 425 psi spec assumes.

    Hazards

    • High-pressure oil out of the open charge valve if the seal leaks. Use a hose to the container and stand clear of the valve while pressure is on.
    • The rear rising, and crush points at the scissor link.
    • Engine running with a nitrogen valve open, which is outside the manual.
    Afterwards: repeat the blowdown test at the next service. If the bottom reads low again or the rear hangs up again, the separator is binding repeatedly and the strut needs a rebuild. Ask Kress whether the lower separator has a stop that keeps it from riding too high.

    Set or reset the rear precharge

    Spec is 425 ±50 psi bottom and 1475 ±50 psi top at 21 °C. Correct for temperature when it's more than 5.5 °C away: target = spec × (°C + 273) ÷ 294. Kress's own table (Table 4) lists 220 and 1200 psi at 21 °C, which doesn't match the spec, so don't use its numbers.

    Why the order matters

    The gauge only tells you how much nitrogen is in there if the lower separator is where Kress assumes: pushed up against the rod end, with the rear fully down. Getting it there needs the oil free to go to tank. If the oil is locked in, or the separator is stuck, the same reading means a different amount of gas. That's how a "low" bottom ends up overcharged.

    Before you start (all three)

    1. Engine off. Kress: don't run the engine while you check the precharge.
    2. Relieve hydraulic system pressure (Operation and Maintenance Manual, System Pressure Release).
    3. Struts fully lowered, then blown down and held open to tank. Use the manual override on the blowdown solenoid (turn and pull to open), or IQAN levelling blowdown with the key on.
    4. Check IQAN: rear at 0% and strut oil at 0 psi. If the rear sits above 0%, stop and use Unstick the separator.
    5. Let the temperature settle for 10 minutes before reading. IQAN's nitrogen readings only count blown down, settled and temperature corrected.
    6. Low-pressure charging assembly 224506 on the bottom only, high-pressure 224500 on the top only. Dry nitrogen (99.8%) from a bottle with a CGA 580 connection. Never oxygen, never an adapter.

    Set or check (raise the charge)

    1. Remove the cap from the charge valve.
    2. Back the gas chuck T-handle all the way out (anticlockwise).
    3. Close the bleed valve on the charging assembly.
    4. Fit the swivel nut to the charge valve at 10–15 in-lb, with no loops or twists in the hose.
    5. Turn the T-handle in until the gauge shows chamber pressure. Don't wind it all the way down or you'll damage the valve core.
    6. Hold the charge valve hex with one spanner and unscrew the poppet hex with another. Three turns is fully open.
    7. Crack the shut-off valve and fill slowly. Stop about 50 psi under the target.
    8. Wait 10–15 minutes for the gas temperature to settle, then top up to target. If you go over, use Lower the charge.
    9. Tighten the poppet hex to close the poppet, 5–8 ft-lb.
    10. Turn the T-handle all the way out, then open the bleed valve.
    11. Hold the charge valve still, loosen the swivel nut and remove the charging assembly.
    12. Refit the cap, 18–27 in-lb.

    Lower the charge

    1. Do steps 1–6 of Set or check.
    2. Open the bleed valve slowly. Never press the valve core with anything, because high pressure can rupture the rubber seat.
    3. Wait 10–15 minutes for the gas temperature to settle, then check again.
    4. At target, finish with steps 9–12 of Set or check.

    Reset from zero (bottom chamber)

    1. Do steps 1–6 of Set or check on the scissor-link charge valve, with the bleed running into an approved container.
    2. Open the bleed valve slowly and let the chamber drain completely. Both rear struts drain together through the linked bottom chambers. Check that both 111183 valves are open.
    3. Watch the oil. A little is normal. If oil keeps flowing, the separator seal is leaking and the cylinder needs repair.
    4. Check IQAN again: rear at 0%, oil at 0 psi, struts still open to tank.
    5. Close the bleed valve and charge from empty with steps 7–12 of Set or check. As the gas goes in, it pushes the separator up to the rod end and the oil goes to tank.
    Finish with the blowdown test. Still engine off and open to tank, the rear should sit at 0% with 0 psi oil. Close the override, start up and let auto level bring it to ride height. Record the settled bottom and top readings and compare them at the next service.
